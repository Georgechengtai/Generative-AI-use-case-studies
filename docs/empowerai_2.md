???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Hannes Peltonen  
    **School:** University of Tampere  
    **Tools used:** Microsoft Copilot, Scopus AI  
    **Events:** A master’s‑level research methods workshop with about 20 students. The class worked in small groups on projects covering five different research methods. Students also visited an archive as part of the course. The focus was on how AI could support group work, research tasks, and reflection.

???+ tip "Implementation"
    Students used AI every week for their group projects. They used it to brainstorm ideas, prepare transcripts, translate material, and create graphics. AI also helped them analyse data and structure their findings.  

    Each group kept a learning portfolio where they reflected on how AI shaped their work. They discussed when AI was useful, when it was unreliable, and how it changed the way they collaborated. The archive visit was included so students could compare hands‑on research with AI‑assisted work.  

    The course leader encouraged students to share experiences openly, so that learning about AI became a collective process rather than an individual trial‑and‑error exercise.

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - Working with peers helps students build AI skills more quickly.  
    - Students must learn to question the reliability and ethics of AI tools, not just their convenience.  
    - Critical thinking remains the central skill, even when AI is used heavily in research tasks.
