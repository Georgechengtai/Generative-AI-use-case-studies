# Beyond EmpowerAI in 2025

This page highlights two selected external 2025 case studies on the use of Generative AI in higher education.  
They are not part of the EmpowerAI consortium but provide additional perspectives on how universities are experimenting with AI in their courses.

!!! quote "Editor's Note"
    These two cases were chosen because they are both representative — and, frankly, among the very few detailed accounts available in 2025 for social sciences. 
    What we do see are individual teachers and students moving quickly, experimenting with frontier models.  
    The pace of change is striking: GPT‑3.5 (the “default” in early 2024) was soon replaced by GPT‑4 for free use, and by 2025 GPT‑5 is already powering many of these initiatives.  
    This shows how **individuals are often ahead of the broader system**, adapting as models evolve.

    Compared with these individual accounts, the **EmpowerAI case studies** provide much richer implementation details and systematic feedback, making them more robust and qualified examples.  
    Our choice to highlight the two cases below reflects both their representativeness and the limited number of well‑documented social science examples currently available.

---

## University of Sydney – School of Economics

**Case:** [Making Peace with AI – ChatGPT‑Integrated Assessments in Economics](https://educational-innovation.sydney.edu.au/teaching@sydney/making-peace-with-ai-chatgpt-integrated-assessments-in-economics/)  
**Department:** School of Economics, Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences  
**Use case:**  
- Large first‑year economics (ECON1002, ~1000 students/semester) and postgraduate capstone (ECON7030, ~200 students/semester).  
- Shifted structured essays and literature reviews into **AI‑human collaboration tasks**, where students had to:  
  - Engage ChatGPT in dialogue on essay/research topics.  
  - Critically evaluate and improve AI outputs.  
  - Demonstrate effective prompt design, information literacy, and ethical awareness.  
- Rubrics were updated to assess “co‑creation with AI.”  
- Students appreciated a **positive, not punitive, approach** to AI integration, though scepticism remained.

## Aberystwyth University – Department of Geography and Earth Sciences

**Case:** [Generative AI in Learning and Teaching: Case Study Series #3](https://wordpress.aber.ac.uk/e-learning/2025/07/11/generative-ai-in-learning-and-teaching-case-study-series-3/)  
**Department:** Department of Geography and Earth Sciences (DGES)  
**Use case:**  
- Third‑year Human Geography/Sociology module *GS37920 Memory Cultures*.  
- Students evaluated an AI‑generated summary of “flashbulb memory” during a lecture.  
- Discussion revealed strong **student GenAI literacy**: they critiqued AI’s repetitive tone, vagueness, and lack of specificity.  
- Helped students reflect on AI’s limitations and usefulness as a **“research assistant.”**  
- Assessment design was updated: generic essay questions replaced with **scenario‑based, applied exam questions** to encourage higher‑level critical thinking.
