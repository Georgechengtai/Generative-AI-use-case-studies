# Assessment - Automated via NotebookLM (Political Science)

!!! quote "Editor's Note"

    We also have a introductory article on[NotebookLM](notebookLM.md), but I encourage you to try it yourself directly.

???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Heiko Pääbo  
    **School:** University of Tartu  
    **Tools used:** NotebookLM, Moodle (with XML import)  
    **Events:** Part of the same **18‑month trial** alongside Case 7, in **MA‑level political science courses**.  
    Courses followed a **flipped classroom model**, where students studied readings and videos before seminars.  
    Challenge: creating effective **self‑study and assessment tools** was very time‑consuming.  
    AI was introduced to generate **practice questions, quizzes, and revision materials** to support independent learning.

???+ tip "Implementation"
    - **Source collection**: Teacher gathered course texts, videos, and webpages. Files were clearly named (e.g., *Democratic_Theory_Reading1.pdf*) so NotebookLM could cite sources.  
    - **Question generation**: NotebookLM produced questions across cognitive levels (recall → evaluation). Teacher specified formats (MCQs, matching, etc.) and required explanations for wrong answers.  
    - **Selection & integration**: Teacher reviewed items, exported them in XML, and uploaded them to Moodle for student practice.  
    - **Exam prep**: NotebookLM also generated revision guides and reflective questions. These outputs informed final exam design.  

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - **Efficiency**: AI reduced time needed to create self‑study materials.  
    - **Consistency**: Ensured systematic coverage of all course topics.  
    - **Scalability**: Workflow can be reused in other courses or scaled to MOOCs.  
    - **Student benefits**: Immediate feedback with source references, self‑paced practice, and reduced exam stress.  
    - **Challenges**:  
      - Occasional factual errors or superficial questions.  
      - AI sometimes skipped sources or produced uneven coverage.  
      - Feedback not always personalised to individual misunderstandings.  
    - **Recommendations**:  
      - Always review AI‑generated questions for accuracy.  
      - Use AI mainly for **formative self‑study**; keep summative evaluation under teacher control.  
      - Refine prompts iteratively; small changes improve quality.  
      - Gather student feedback to check that learning outcomes are met.  
      - Balance automation with manual grading for deeper analytical tasks.
