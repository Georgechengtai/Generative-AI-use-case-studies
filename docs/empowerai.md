# Intro to EmpowerAI Case Studies

## What is EmpowerAI
The **EmpowerAI Consortium** is a Nordic‑Baltic collaboration supporting social sciences and humanities teachers in the responsible and ethical use of AI in teaching and learning.  
One of its main outputs is the **[Guide: Practical Case Studies](https://sisu.ut.ee/empowerai/guide/)**, which presents real classroom experiments with AI carried out by partner universities.  

## How to read this subsection

Here you can:  

- Browse each case study from EmpowerAI individually,  
- Compare practices across different classes/professors in University of Turka, University of Tampere and Kanuas University of Technology
- Use the examples as inspiration for your own teaching.  


## 📊 Case Studies Theme

| **Title** | **Author(s)** | **Tools Used** | **When / What / How / Class info** | **Implementation** | **Key Take‑aways** |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|-------------------------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| *Promoting Critical Thinking with AI-based Tools* | Outi Veivo, Jere Riekkinen & Leena Salmi (University of Turku) | ChatGPT | Survey of 27 teachers and PhD students (Humanities, Social Sciences, AI Academy, AInspiration community). | Activities grouped into 5 areas: debate AI, compare AI vs. textbooks, reflect on prompts, trace citations, discuss ethics/bias. | Design tasks for reflection and critique. Students need to question AI outputs. AI literacy is now a core skill. |
| *“Hive-minding” AI in Higher Education* | Hannes Peltonen (University of Tampere) | Microsoft Copilot, Scopus AI | Master’s-level research methods workshop, ~20 students, weekly projects across 5 methods + archive visit. | Group projects with AI: brainstorming, transcripts, translations, graphics, analysis, portfolio reflections. | Peer learning builds AI skills. Ethics and reliability must be addressed. Critical thinking stays central. |
| *Insights from Workshop “AI in Bachelor Thesis Writing”* | Josefina Sipinen (University of Tampere) | LLMs (general) | Feb 2025 pilot workshop, Bachelor’s thesis writing (20–30 pages). | 2-hour workshop: intro, hands-on AI for research Qs, frameworks, analysis planning, group reflection. | AI helps break thesis work into steps. Oversight is needed. AI literacy and clear rules reduce uncertainty. |
| *Integration of AI into Classroom Activities* | Yurii Kondratyk (University of Tartu) | ChatGPT, MS Teams transcription | MA course in Central & East European, Russian, Eurasian Studies, 38 students. | Group projects, debates, reflection templates, SAMR model, text analysis comparing AI vs. human-like outputs. | Reflection tasks reveal student use. Debates are engaging. Transcription saves time but raises privacy concerns. |
| *How to Enhance IR Simulations with an AI Tool?* | Heiko Pääbo (University of Tartu) | ChatGPT | 20 students (2023) and 14 students (2024), online via Moodle forums. | AI created branching crisis scenarios, multi-genre texts, propaganda tones, analysed student forum posts. | Adds realism and adaptability. AI avoids conflict. Visuals limited. Prompts need to be precise. |
| *How AI Summaries Help Students Prepare for Exams* | Greta Danilavičienė (Kaunas University of Technology) | ChatGPT 4.0, MS Word transcription, Zoom | Spring 2025, 9 students, oral communication course with presentations. | Presentations recorded → transcribed → processed with AI into summaries for oral exam prep. | Supports equity and inclusivity. Quality depends on transcripts. Tool access matters. |
| *AI as Grading Assistant (Writing Assistance)* | Heiko Pääbo (University of Tartu) | ChatGPT (preferred), Copilot, CoGrader (unreliable) | MA courses, 15–30 students, 18 months. | AI drafted feedback using parallel, refinement, and rubric-guided methods. Submissions anonymised. | Saves time and improves consistency. Transparency and human oversight are essential. |
| *AI as Grading Assistant (Automated)* | Heiko Pääbo (University of Tartu) | NotebookLM, Moodle | Postgraduate political science, flipped classroom, 18 months. | AI generated self-study questions, XML upload to Moodle, revision guides for exams. | Efficient and scalable. Risks include inaccuracies, superficial questions, and student over-reliance. |
