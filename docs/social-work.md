

## Introduction

![social work](images/social_work.png)

Social work, as a discipline dedicated to enhancing human well-being and meeting basic human needs, is increasingly exploring the integration of artificial intelligence (AI) in its practice and education. The field recognizes AI's potential to augment social workers' capabilities in areas such as case management, risk assessment, and service delivery. However, it also grapples with ethical considerations surrounding AI use, particularly in maintaining the human-centered approach that is fundamental to social work. As the profession evolves, social work education is adapting to prepare future practitioners for an AI-integrated landscape while emphasising the irreplaceable nature of human empathy and professional judgment.

## Potential AI Applications in Teaching and Learning

- **VR training or cultural immersion experience**: Virtual reality simulations for social work students to practice interventions or experience diverse cultural contexts, enhancing empathy and cultural competence.
- **Virtual Patients**: Expansion of VR and AI technologies to create more dynamic and responsive simulations of client interactions, allowing students to practice a wide range of scenarios and interventions.[^4]
- **Summarising and Brainstorming**: Utilisation of AI tools like ChatGPT for generating and analysing case studies, policy proposals, and intervention strategies, enhancing students' critical thinking and problem-solving skills. [^5]
- **Grading and Feedback**: Integration of AI in peer review processes for student assignments and creation of AI-assisted study guides, offering more frequent and detailed feedback to students. [^6]
- **AI Mentor**: A chatbot with pre-programmed prompts and a knowledge base to answer common questions about social work concepts, theories, and practices, providing students with quick access to basic information and guidance. [^7]

## Practical Cases of AI application

### 1. Virtual Reality Simulations at University of Kentucky[^1]

#### Description

The University of Kentucky's College of Social Work has implemented virtual reality (VR) simulations for child welfare investigation training. This technology creates immersive learning experiences using 360-degree video and computer-generated virtual reality. Students wear VR goggles to virtually assess home environments and investigate potential cases of neglect or abuse, preparing them for real-world scenarios in a safe, controlled setting.

#### Testimonies 

Dr. Justin "Jay" Miller, dean of Kentucky's College of Social Work, stated: 
> "We really started to lean into looking at different ways to prepare students; a lot of times people can learn just by delivering content in different ways. We do a lot of virtual reality simulations, which is really a good way for students to gain the experience they need before they get out into other contexts."

#### Effects/Interpretation

This approach enhances student preparedness by providing realistic, hands-on experience without the risks associated with real-world training. It allows for repeated practice and immediate feedback, potentially improving learning outcomes.

### 2. AI-Enhanced Curriculum at Touro University[^2]

#### Description

Dr. Jamie Sundvall, the Online Master of Social Work Program Director at Touro University Graduate School of Social Work, has integrated various AI tools into their curriculum. These include decision-making trees with programmed virtual patients, VR simulations for community observation, AI-powered administrative platforms, and the use of ChatGPT for various educational purposes.

#### Testimonies 

Dr. Sundvall reports that these AI tools reduce student anxiety about being observed and graded during patient interactions. She states, 
> "By using AI in training, we can help students develop evidence-based clinical judgment in a controlled environment before they ever start practicing."

#### Effects/Interpretation

This comprehensive approach to AI integration in social work education provides students with a diverse range of experiences and skills, from clinical decision-making to administrative tasks, better preparing them for the evolving landscape of social work practice.

### 3. AI-Assisted Course Preparation at University of Texas at Arlington [^3]

#### Description

Prof. Karen Magruder, an assistant professor of practice at the School of Social Work, has incorporated ChatGPT into her teaching methods. She uses the AI tool to generate case studies for discussion in her graduate cognitive behavioural therapy course and assigns students to critically evaluate AI-generated information.

#### Testimonies 

Prof. Magruder states: 
> "It definitely can be alarming to maybe think about some of the implications for this development, but there's also a lot of opportunities and ways that we can harness this for the social good in the classroom and beyond."

#### Effects/Interpretation

This approach not only enhances course content but also teaches students to critically engage with AI-generated information, a crucial skill in the modern information landscape.

## Other Examples

- [Crisis Contact Simulator](https://www.thetrevorproject.org/blog/the-trevor-project-launches-new-ai-tool-to-support-crisis-counselor-training): The Trevor Project, supported by Google, has developed a tool that simulates conversations with LGBTQ+ youths in crisis to train counsellors.
- AI Chatbots for Counselling Support: In reverse, [AI-powered chatbots](https://www.thesocialworkgraduate.com/post/artificial-intelligence). can provide initial counselling or emotional support, particularly for individuals hesitant to seek traditional therapy or with limited access to social work services 
- AI-powered virtual therapy assistants offering support between sessions and practicing coping strategies with clients.
- Language pattern analysis tools to help social workers identify themes and sentiments in client communications.
- AI-assisted documentation tools for streamlining case notes, treatment plans, and progress reports.
- Automated policy analysis systems to synthesise complex documents and identify gaps in existing policies.
- AI-enhanced qualitative data analysis for program evaluations, identifying themes and patterns from interviews and focus groups.
- Culturally-sensitive message generation for community organising and outreach campaigns.
- Real-time translation tools to facilitate international social work and cross-cultural communication.
