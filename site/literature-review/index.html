    Wide usage of LLMs in diverse educational NLP applications.

    • Natural Language Processing (NLP) refers to the branch of computer science and artificial intelligence, that empowers computers the ability to understand text and spoken words as the way human beings can.
    • NLP techniques have significantly revolutionized various domains, including healthcare, commerce, and notably, education.
    • The survey by Lan et al. (2023) highlights the transformative impact of NLP in educational settings, where it aids in tasks such as automated assessment, error correction, and intelligent tutoring. The integration of NLP in education fosters personalized learning experiences, enhances student engagement, and streamlines administrative tasks.
    • Building on this foundation, recent advancements in AI have introduced generative models like ChatGPT, which serve as virtual teaching assistants in programming education, providing personalized assistance and clarifying complex concepts (Liu & M'hiri, 2024). Additionally, LLM-assisted human evaluation experiments show that novice graders can achieve expert-level accuracy, enhancing grading efficiency (Xiao, 2024). AI-driven personalized learning experiences further adapt to individual student needs, fostering dynamic and adaptive learning environments (Wu, 2024).
    • Moreover, the introduction of accessible generative artificial intelligence opens promising opportunities for personalized learning methods in education, as a tool that generates learning materials in multiple formats and includes progress-assessment quizzes, showing increased student engagement and study time, though findings are preliminary due to a small sample size (Pesovski, 2024).

    Potential Use Cases in Social Science Environment

    Using No-Code AI to Harness the Power of Machine Learning Data Analysis

    • Leif Su and Jonny Holmstrom (2024) explore the application of a no-code AI platform within an educational framework that emphasizes problem-centered learning, activation, demonstration, application, and integration. This framework aims to make machine learning (ML) accessible to students from diverse educational backgrounds who may not have technical expertise. By leveraging no-code AI tools, the authors demonstrate how students can engage with ML concepts and create value from data without needing extensive programming skills.

    Quick and Efficient Analysis on Qualitative Data in Social Science Research

    • Ion Lixandru (2024) presents a compelling case for using generative AI tools like ChatGPT in social science research. The study highlights how ChatGPT can significantly accelerate the process of qualitative data analysis. By analyzing data from a focus group of 8 respondents, the study found that the instructed AI-generated analysis closely matched traditional methods. This demonstrates that AI can be a reliable tool for interpreting qualitative information. The AI’s synthesis capability allows researchers to pre-process complex raw texts efficiently, making it a valuable asset in the current academic and research context.

    Brief Mention of Other Use Cases

    The following examples illustrate how AI is making significant strides across various educational disciplines. This list is not exhaustive and only includes papers collected from open-source rather than proprietary content. - Showcasing a Functional Outcome in Game Development: Fiona French (2023) explores the integration of OpenAI tools like ChatGPT and DALL-E in game development education at London Metropolitan University. A cohort of games programming students was tasked with evaluating these tools in a research and development assignment. They demonstrated working solutions and reported their findings, showcasing outputs that enhanced their programming, problem-solving, and exploratory design skills. - AI-Assisted Creation of Diverse Medical Case Vignettes: M. Bakkum (2023) demonstrates how AI can accelerate the creation of diverse medical case scenarios, offering inclusive educational resources for medical training. - AI Generative Art in 3D Design Studio Art Course: James Hutson & Bryan Robertson (2023) presents a study on integrating AI generative art tools in a traditional 3D design studio art course. The research explores how AI tools like Craiyon and DALLE-2 can enhance creative workflows and prompt engineering. Students were tasked with using these AI tools to generate verbal cues, combining three different objects into new 3D sculptures or models. - AI Educational Video Assistant Modules: Rana Al-Shaikh (2024) investigates the integration of AI tools like OpenAI's Whisper and Google's Bard to enhance educational videos through transcription, engagement, and reinforcement techniques.
