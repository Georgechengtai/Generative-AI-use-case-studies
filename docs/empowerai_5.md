# Simulations (International Relations)

!!! quote "Editor's Note"

    For further information on role-playing, you may check our [introductory article](role-playing.md).

???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Heiko Pääbo  
    **School:** University of Tartu  
    **Tools used:** ChatGPT 4.0, Copilot, Moodle forums  
    **Events:** Crisis simulations run with 20 students in 2023 and 14 students in 2024 within the Master's programme in **International Relations and Regional Studies**.
    Both years the simulations were conducted online via Moodle forums, where students debated, negotiated, and made decisions.  
    AI was integrated to enrich scenarios, generate diverse perspectives, and provide more realistic communication materials.

???+ tip "Implementation"
    - **Scenario generation**: AI created crisis events with multiple possible developments, adjusting to student decisions in real time.  
    - **Genre adaptivity**: Produced outputs in different forms (media reports, government communiqués, diplomatic notes, expert interviews) with varied tones, including propaganda and biased messaging.  
    - **Simulation enrichment**: AI generated fictional characters, speeches, and multilingual materials to mirror the complexity of real-world politics.  
    - **Interaction analysis**: AI assisted in reviewing Moodle forum posts, summarising discussion trends, and highlighting mismatches between actors’ intentions and actions.

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - Student engagement increased when scenarios were dynamic and multi‑path instead of purely linear.  
    - AI outputs often showed a **conflict‑avoiding bias**, tending toward compromise, so careful prompting and editing were needed.  
    - Early AI systems resisted fabricating political/news content unless clearly framed as part of a simulation.  
    - AI tools struggled with realistic visuals; existing media sources remained necessary.  
    - Effective use requires **clear prompts** that specify scenario objectives, tone, and perspective.  
    - Teachers should always **review and refine AI outputs** rather than using them directly.  
    - Framing the exercise explicitly as **fictional and educational** avoids ethical and accuracy issues.
