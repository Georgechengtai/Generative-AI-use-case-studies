# Exam preparation (broad-based)

!!! quote "Editor's Note"

    I personally would recommended speech-to-text model like OpenAI's Whisper though (a simply way to access it is though MacWhisper in Macbook, but you could of course use it in Window system). A one-stop approach is generally better than using multiple tools sequentally, but you could experiment the below approach freely.


???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Greta Danilavičienė  
    **School:** Kaunas University of Technology  
    **Tools used:** Microsoft Word transcription, Zoom transcription, ChatGPT 4.0  
    **Events:** In **Spring 2025**, a course on *Technical and Professional Communication in English* with **9 students** (1 from the School of Economics and Business, 8 from Architecture and Civil Engineering).  
    Students gave oral presentations based on ~70 pages of academic reading. Presentations were both in‑person and via Zoom.  
    Problem: missed classes, unclear speech (due to accents, orthodontic devices, or recording quality), and hybrid formats made comprehension and exam prep difficult.  

???+ tip "Implementation"
    - **Recording & transcription**:  
      - In‑person presentations recorded on phone → transcribed manually with Microsoft Word.  
      - Zoom presentations benefitted from automatic transcription.  
    - **AI summarisation**:  
      - All transcripts were processed in ChatGPT 4.0 with prompts designed to extract concise, structured summaries.  
      - Summaries highlighted main arguments even when speech was unclear.  
    - **Workflow**:  
      1. Record presentation.  
      2. Transcribe (via Word or Zoom).  
      3. Summarise with ChatGPT.  
      4. Review and refine summaries.  
    - **Teacher responsibility**:  
      - The teacher handled recording, transcription, and AI processing.  
      - Summaries were anonymised before uploading.  
      - Oral exam questions could be based on the content of these presentations.

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - AI‑generated summaries ensured **fair access** to content for all students, regardless of attendance or clarity of speech.  
    - Students valued the summaries as study aids for preparing oral exams.  
    - Even strong speakers benefited: summaries simplified review and reinforced comprehension.  
    - **Challenges**:  
      - Microsoft Word transcription limited to Windows.  
      - Summary quality depended heavily on input quality (clear audio).  
      - Prompt design required practice and teacher training.  
    - **Recommendations**:  
      - Provide **AI literacy training** so students can eventually manage summarisation themselves.  
      - Universities should supply reliable transcription tools.  
      - AI should **supplement, not replace** oral presentation practice.  
      - Consider institutional licenses for AI tools to support inclusive learning.
