# Classroom activities (Regional Studies)

!!! quote "Editor's Note"

    This approach is like: you might (or should) use AI, it's okay, but we would make sure you don't abuse. To think of how you think.

???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Yurii Kondratyk  
    **School:** University of Tartu  
    **Tools used:** ChatGPT, Perplexity, Microsoft Teams (transcription)  
    **Events:** A Master’s‑level course *Introduction to Central & East European, Russian, and Eurasian Studies* with 38 students.  
    Students created group video projects for exam preparation and shared research proposals anonymously for peer review.  
    AI was introduced as part of regular classwork, framed around three competencies of critical AI use:  
    1. Students do not outsource ideas to AI.  
    2. Students verify AI outputs.  
    3. Students recognize biases and algorithmic influence.

???+ tip "Implementation"
    - Students completed a **weekly reflection template**, answering how they used AI, how useful it was, and how they dealt with misleading results.  
    - They held **debates on AI in academia**, weighing pros and cons with attention to ethics, bias, and academic integrity.  
    - **Microsoft Teams transcription** supported group discussions, freeing students to focus on content and peer feedback.  
    - The **SAMR framework** (Substitution, Augmentation, Modification, Redefinition) guided reflection on AI’s role in learning.
    - In a **text analysis exercise**, students compared two AI‑generated texts (one written to sound “human”), then discussed how difficult it was to distinguish AI from human writing.

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - Reflection templates are effective when used sparingly; overuse makes them routine.  
    - Debates are low‑prep but highly engaging for students.  
    - Transcription tools save time but must respect privacy; only trusted university platforms (like Teams) should be used.  
    - Text analysis tasks need enough discussion time — the real learning happens when students talk through AI’s limits and biases.
