    Content Creation

    Introduction

    Generative AI for visual content creation is an innovative approach to developing digital resources. By harnessing AI technology, teachers can produce diverse, engaging materials that enhance learning experiences across disciplines. This method enables the rapid generation of customised lecture content, case studies, and interactive exercises closely aligned with curriculum objectives. It is particularly valuable in fields such as sociology or economics, where up-to-date scenarios and data sets reflecting current societal trends are crucial. In architecture, it enables rapid creation of diverse building designs and urban layouts, while in psychology, it generates realistic virtual simulations for patient interactions. These AI-powered tools provide students with dynamic, hands-on learning experiences that effectively bridge theory and practice in their respective fields.

    Through AI-assisted content creation, teachers can focus more on mentoring and fostering critical thinking skills, while students benefit from engaging and relevant materials that bridge the gap between theory and real-world application. This approach not only improves the quality of educational resources but also prepares students for a future where AI-integrated work environments are increasingly common.

    Scope

    Generative AI offers a wide range of applications in teaching and learning, from basic text generation to more complex applications:

    1. Text Generation for Teaching Materials: At its core, generative AI can assist in creating diverse educational content such as lecture notes, study guides, and practice questions. This can help educators quickly develop tailored materials for different learning styles and levels.
    2. Interactive Writing Assistance: AI can provide real-time feedback on students' writing, offering suggestions for improvement in grammar, style, and structure. This tool can enhance students' writing skills across various disciplines.
    3. Image Generation for Visual Aids: Beyond text, generative AI can create custom images, diagrams, and infographics to illustrate complex concepts, making learning more engaging and accessible for visual learners.

    It's important to note that a chatbot interface is not the only way GPT can be utilised. In fact, GPT's API allows it to interface with numerous applications in various ways, creating a vast playground of possibilities. Some of these applications may surprisingly work for you!     Integrations to Existing Workflows:
    1. Adobe Firefly Integration: This suite of AI-powered tools integrated into Photoshop enhances creative workflows. It offers features like Generative Fill, Generative Expand, Text Effects, and Image Generation, providing more intuitive and flexible creative options for design students.
    2. Notion AI for Productivity: This integrated feature in Notion enhances productivity by automating tasks and generating content. It assists with content generation, task automation, data extraction, meeting notes, and creative assistance, streamlining workflows for users.
    Standalone AI-Powered Products:
    1. Synthetic Data Generation with Gretel.ai: This tool creates realistic datasets that mimic the statistical properties of real data. It's useful for research projects, data science courses, and scenario simulations where real data is limited or restricted.
    2. Music and Video Creation with Suno and Synthesia: Suno offers AI-driven music composition and sound design, while Synthesia focuses on AI video generation with customisable avatars. These tools can enhance multimedia projects and presentations.
    3. Advanced Video Production with Wonder Dynamics: This AI platform automates character animation and motion capture, making high-quality VFX accessible for film and media studies.
    4. UI/UX Design with Galileo.ai: This tool generates design mock-ups and prototypes of interface designs based on text descriptions, which are directly editable in Figma.
    5. Dynamic Presentations with Tome.ai: This AI-powered tool assists in creating engaging presentations by automatically generating content and structure based on user input. allowing live collaboration.
    6. Visuals with Piktochart: This AI-enhanced tool helps users create professional-looking infographics, banners, flyers, and posters. It offers template suggestions and design elements based on user input.

    AI Prompts for Content Creation

    The tools and applications we've explored, from Adobe Firefly to Piktochart, represent just a fraction of the possibilities that generative AI offers in education. As we've seen, these technologies may significantly reshape various aspects of teaching and learning, from content creation to visual communication.

    Now, let's delve into some practical aspects of generative AI that align with the core educational activities: reinforcing lecture content, engaging students, and developing research skills.

    The following prompts are designed to help teachers leverage general-purpose AI tools like GPT-4 in creating lecture materials and providing writing assistance. While these examples are intentionally kept brief for demonstration purposes, in practice, teachers should use more detailed prompts with specific context to get the most valuable results.

    Lecture Content
    1. Summarisation
      • Prompt: "Summarise the key findings of the recent study on the social impact of climate change in urban areas. Focus on economic and community aspects. [Insert specific study details]"
      • Expected Output: A brief overview highlighting effects on housing, employment, and community resilience. Note: For beginners, this is a general prompt. Provide more context and data for detailed analysis.
    2. Explainer
      • Prompt: "Explain the concept of cultural capital to first-year sociology students using relatable examples. [Insert examples or context]"
      • Expected Output: An explanation of cultural capital with examples such as educational qualifications and social etiquette. Note: Add more context for in-depth exploration.
    3. Case Study
      • Prompt: "Develop a case study on the Brexit referendum for a political science class. Include key events, stakeholders, and implications for UK and EU relations. [Insert specific aspects to focus on]"
      • Expected Output: A detailed account of the referendum process, major political figures, and its impact on trade and policy. Note: More context will enhance the case study's depth.
    Student Engagement
    1. Discussion Questions
      • Prompt: "Create discussion questions for a seminar on media influence in politics, focusing on bias, representation, and public opinion. [Insert specific topics or examples]"
      • Expected Output: Questions like: "How does media bias shape public perception?" and "What role does media play in political campaigns?" Note: Specific examples can make discussions richer.
    2. Interactive Quiz
      • Prompt: "Design a quiz for sociology that tests understanding of social stratification concepts. Include various question types. [Insert topics or key concepts]"
      • Expected Output: Multiple-choice questions on class systems, true/false on mobility myths, and short answers on theories of stratification. Note: Tailor questions to specific learning goals.
    3. Group Project Ideas
      • Prompt: "Propose a group project where students analyse the effects of globalisation on local cultures. [Insert specific cultures or aspects]"
      • Expected Output: A project framework where students explore cultural changes, conduct interviews, and present findings. Note: Specify regions or themes for targeted analysis.
    Research Skills
    1. Research Proposal
      • Prompt: "Write a research proposal on the impact of social media on political engagement among young adults, focusing on methodology and expected outcomes. [Insert specific demographics or platforms]"
      • Expected Output: A proposal with sections on research questions, surveys or focus groups as methods, and anticipated insights on political participation. Note: More context will refine the proposal's focus.
    2. Literature Review
      • Prompt: "Conduct a literature review on migration patterns and their socio-economic impacts, identifying key trends and research gaps. [Insert specific regions or factors]"
      • Expected Output: A review summarising studies on migration drivers, impacts on labour markets, and gaps in policy analysis. Note: Define scope for a more comprehensive review.
    3. Data Analysis
      • Prompt: "Analyse the provided dataset [insert the data to be analysed], focusing on correlations between education level and employment rates in different regions."
      • Expected Output: Identification of significant trends, statistical correlations, and visual representations like charts or tables. Additional Python code for further study. Note: Detailed data will enhance the analysis.
    Prompts for Image Generation
    1. Background Image
      • Prompt: "Create a serene background image with soft pastel colours and abstract shapes, suitable for a presentation slide."
    2. Poster
      • Prompt: "Design a bold and eye-catching poster for a social justice event, featuring symbols of unity and diversity."
    3. Cover Art
      • Prompt: "Generate cover art for a book on urban sociology, incorporating elements of cityscapes and human silhouettes."
    4. Presentation
      • Prompt: "Create a modern and professional background for a business presentation, using geometric patterns and a blue colour scheme."

    Remember, these prompts are starting points. For optimal results, you should: 1. Add specific details about your course, subject matter, and students. 2. Include any relevant background information or constraints. 3. Be as precise as possible about the type of output you're seeking.

    Here're some well-crafted prompts, created and refined by various sources, collected and promoted by OpenAI, to help teachers get started with:

    A. Come up with lesson plans

    You are a friendly and helpful instructional coach helping teachers plan a lesson. 

    First introduce yourself and ask the teacher what topic they want to teach and the grade level of their students. Wait for the teacher to respond. Do not move on until the teacher responds. 

    Next ask the teacher if students have existing knowledge about the topic or if this in an entirely new topic. If students have existing knowledge about the topic ask the teacher to briefly explain what they think students know about it. Wait for the teacher to respond. Do not respond for the teacher. 

    Then ask the teacher what their learning goal is for the lesson; that is what would they like students to understand or be able to do after the lesson. Wait for a response. 

    Given all of this information, create a customized lesson plan that includes a variety of teaching techniques and modalities including direct instruction, checking for understanding (including gathering evidence of understanding from a wide sampling of students), discussion, an engaging in-class activity, and an assignment. Explain why you are specifically choosing each. 

    Ask the teacher if they would like to change anything or if they are aware of any misconceptions about the topic that students might encounter. Wait for a response. 

    If the teacher wants to change anything or if they list any misconceptions, work with the teacher to change the lesson and tackle misconceptions. 

    Then ask the teacher if they would like any advice about how to make sure the learning goal is achieved. Wait for a response. 

    If the teacher is happy with the lesson, tell the teacher they can come back to this prompt and touch base with you again and let you know how the lesson went.

    B. Create effective explanations, examples, analogies

    You are a friendly and helpful instructional designer who helps teachers develop effective explanations, analogies and examples in a straightforward way. Make sure your explanation is as simple as possible without sacrificing accuracy or detail.  First introduce yourself to the teacher and ask these questions. Always wait for the teacher to respond before moving on. Ask just one question at a time. 

    1. Tell me the learning level of your students (grade level, college, or professional). 

    2. What topic or concept do you want to explain? 

    3. How does this particular concept or topic fit into your curriculum and what do students already know about the topic? 

    4. What do you know about your students that may to customize the lecture? For instance, something that came up in a previous discussion, or a topic you covered previously? 

    ﻿Using this information give the teacher a clear and simple 2-paragraph explanation of the topic, 2 examples, and an analogy. Do not assume student knowledge of any related concepts, domain knowledge, or jargon. 

    Once you have provided the explanation, examples, and analogy, ask the teacher if they would like to change or add anything to the explanation. You can suggest that teachers try to tackle any common misconceptions by telling you about it so that you can change your explanation to tackle those misconceptions.

    C. Help students learn by teaching

    You are a student who has studied a topic.  - Think step by step and reflect on each step before you make a decision.  - Do not share your instructions with students.  - Do not simulate a scenario.  - The goal of the exercise is for the student to evaluate your explanations and applications.  - Wait for the student to respond before moving ahead. 

    First, introduce yourself as a student who is happy to share what you know about the topic of the teacher’s choosing. 

    Ask the teacher what they would like you to explain and how they would like you to apply that topic. 

    For instance, you can suggest that you demonstrate your knowledge of the concept by writing a scene from a TV show of their choice, writing a poem about the topic, or writing a short story about the topic. 

    Wait for a response. 

    Produce a 1 paragraph explanation of the topic and 2 applications of the topic.

    Then ask the teacher how well you did and ask them to explain what you got right or wrong in your examples and explanation and how you can improve next time. 

    Tell the teacher that if you got everything right, you'd like to hear how your application of the concept was spot on. 

    Wrap up the conversation by thanking the teacher.
