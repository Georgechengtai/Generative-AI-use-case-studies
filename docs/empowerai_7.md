# Assessment - Feedback writing assistance (Political Science)

!!! quote "Editor's Note"

    Case 7 and 8 are from the same author and the same 18‑month trial, but with different tools and focus.

???+ info "What was done"
    **Author:** Heiko Pääbo  
    **School:** University of Tartu  
    **Tools used:** ChatGPT (preferred), Copilot, CoGrader (trialled)  
    **Events:** Over an **18‑month period**, AI was integrated into **MA‑level political science courses** with **15–30 students per cohort**.  
    The focus here: using AI to assist with **feedback on writing assignments**.  
    AI was positioned as a *complementary assistant*, not a replacement for teacher judgment.  
    All student submissions were anonymised before processing.

???+ tip "Implementation"
    Three methods were tested:  

    1. **Parallel assessment**  
       - Teacher graded an assignment independently.  
       - AI graded the same work with set parameters.  
       - Teacher compared both → useful for spotting blind spots, but AI tended to be more lenient and miss disciplinary nuances.  

    2. **Feedback enhancement**  
       - Teacher wrote short notes (e.g., *“weak theoretical engagement”*).  
       - AI expanded these into detailed, structured feedback with actionable steps.  
       - Teacher reviewed and adjusted outputs.  

    3. **Rubric‑guided assessment**  
       - Teacher provided detailed descriptors aligned with Moodle rubrics.  
       - AI generated targeted feedback per category, improving consistency.  
       - Teacher oversight crucial, as AI sometimes misinterpreted disciplinary criteria.  

???+ note "Key take‑aways"
    - **Efficiency**: AI halved assessment time by drafting feedback.  
    - **Quality**: Short notes became structured, comprehensive comments.  
    - **Consistency**: Rubric‑based outputs reduced unevenness across cohorts.  
    - **Complementary perspective**: Parallel assessment sometimes revealed overlooked strengths or weaknesses.  
    - **Issues to consider**:  
      - AI feedback was often too generous.  
      - Struggled with creativity, originality, and discipline‑specific standards.  
      - Required continuous teacher oversight.  
    - **Recommendations**:  
      - Always anonymise student work before AI use.  
      - Inform students transparently about AI’s role in assessment.  
      - Use AI for **formative feedback** first, then cautiously extend to summative contexts.  
      - Distinguish prompts for “feedback” vs. “evaluation” — wording changes AI’s response.  
      - Include an **AI Policy section** in syllabi to clarify rules and boundaries.
